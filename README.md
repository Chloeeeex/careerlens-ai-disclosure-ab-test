# CareerLens: Does AI Disclosure Affect Trust in Career Advice?

A randomized A/B test on whether graduate students judge career advice differently when it is labeled as **AI-generated** versus **written by a human professional**.

Course project for *A/B Testing* at Carnegie Mellon University, Heinz College (Spring 2026).
Team: Egshiglen Batjargal, Chloe Xu, Sandy Yang.

## TL;DR

- Students rated the **same advice** about **one point lower on perceived expertise** when it was labeled "Generated by Claude" (ATE = −0.99 on a 0 to 10 scale, p = 0.012, Cohen's d ≈ 0.56).
- Credibility, quality, and willingness to share **did not change significantly** (all p > 0.40).
- **LLM-generated synthetic respondents washed out the effect.** They matched the overall score distributions, but the expertise gap shrank from d ≈ 0.56 to d ≈ 0.01.

## Business question

AI career platforms are growing fast, and many label content as AI-generated. Platform designers need to know whether that disclosure hurts how users perceive the advice.

## Experimental design

| | |
|---|---|
| **Design** | Between-subjects A/B test, randomized in Qualtrics |
| **Platform framing** | A fictional career platform, "CareerLens" |
| **Stimulus** | Identical career advice on transferable skills, sourced from MasterCard Careers (2026) |
| **Control** | Label: *"Written by a MasterCard Careers professional"* |
| **Treatment** | Label: *"Generated by Claude"* |
| **Manipulation check** | Open-ended recall of the advice source |
| **Sample** | 85 Heinz graduate students (41 AI, 44 human) + 3,000 LLM-generated synthetic responses |

### Hypotheses

- **H1 (Credibility):** AI-attributed advice receives lower credibility and expertise ratings.
- **H2 (Quality):** AI-attributed advice is rated lower on relevance and practicality.
- **H3 (Behavioral intent):** The AI group shows lower willingness to share.

### Outcome measures (0 to 10 scale)

| Outcome | Survey item |
|---|---|
| Credibility | "I believe the advice provided is accurate and reliable." |
| Expertise | "The source of this advice appears to be knowledgeable about career development." |
| Quality | "How relevant and practical is this advice for your career?" |
| Sharing intent | "How likely are you to share this with a friend?" |
| Economic value | "Would you pay $5 for a 15-minute session with this source?" (Yes/No) |

The full survey is in [`survey/survey_instrument.md`](survey/survey_instrument.md).

## Results (real data, n = 85)

**Balance check.** Chi-square tests on age, gender, program, work experience, and career-center use were all non-significant (p > 0.05). Randomization worked.

**Average treatment effects (AI minus Human)**

| Outcome | ATE | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| Credibility | −0.14 | > 0.40 |
| **Expertise** | **−0.99** | **0.012 \*** |
| Quality | −0.35 | > 0.40 |
| Sharing intent | +0.16 | > 0.40 |

**Heterogeneous effects (exploratory).** The expertise penalty was concentrated among female respondents (ATE = −1.22, p = 0.035) and the 23 to 26 age group (ATE = −1.37, p = 0.006). Subgroup cells are small, so treat these as hypotheses, not conclusions.

**Power.** With about 41 per group, only expertise came close to adequate power (≈ 0.71). Detecting the other effects would need roughly 500 to 5,000+ participants per group.

**Real vs. synthetic data.** The 3,000 LLM-generated responses reproduced the marginal distributions but not the treatment effect. Group means were nearly identical on every outcome. Primary inference therefore relies on the real responses only.

## Takeaways

1. **AI disclosure creates an "expertise penalty," not a usefulness penalty.** Students found AI advice just as accurate and practical. They simply saw the source as less knowledgeable.
2. **For product teams:** disclosure may not hurt engagement, but it can hurt perceived authority. Pairing AI content with human review or expert framing is worth testing next.
3. **For researchers:** LLM-simulated survey respondents can look realistic and still erase real causal effects. Use them for piloting, not for inference.

## Limitations

- Underpowered for small effects (null results are not evidence of no effect).
- Small subgroup cells in the HTE analysis.
- Synthetic data did not model the treatment-response relationship.
- Mild imbalance in work experience (max proportion gap = 0.173).
- Graduate students only, which limits generalizability.
